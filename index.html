Historical Maritime Narrative

The Three Ships of Captain George C. McAllister

How the Great Barbados Hurricane of 1831 Destroyed the *Keziah*, the Rescue by the *Emerald*, and the Commissioning of the *Woodbine*

Original Command 1831

Brig KEZIAH

Master: George C. McAllister (St. Andrews, NB)

  • • Voyage 1 (Jan–May 1831): Carried cedar shingles from Upton's Wharf to Barbados, Tobago, and Trinidad; returned with sugar & molasses via Mona Passage to Halifax.
  • • Voyage 2 (July–Aug 1831): Returned to Barbados with lumber.
  • • The Catastrophe (Aug 10–11, 1831): Driven ashore on Needhams Point, Carlisle Bay in the Great Hurricane. Surveyed and condemned as a total wreck on Aug 15.
Rescue Vessel Sept 1831

Brig EMERALD

Master: Captain Smith (Passage to NB)

  • • Rescue Agreement: Stranded in Barbados, McAllister paid Capt. Smith $100 to carry himself, his surviving crew, and salvaged gear back to St. Andrews.
  • • Homeward Run: Sailed Sept 1, 1831 past Martinique & Deseada Island, crossing the Atlantic back to Passamaquoddy Bay.
  • • Coppered Bottom: McAllister praised her speed: "goes as fast under her topsails as the Keziah did under all that could be crowded upon her."
New Command 1832–1833

Brig WOODBINE

Master: George C. McAllister (~189.5 Tons)

  • • Acquisition & Refit: Acquired in early 1832 in New Brunswick; thoroughly recorked, refitted, and renamed Woodbine by McAllister.
  • • 1832 Barbados Run: Cleared St. Andrews in May 1832; arrived Carlisle Bay Aug 9, 1832; returned to NB in September.
  • • 1833 Barbados Run: Completed another round-trip cargo voyage, discharging sugar at St. Stephen in June 1833 ($7/cwt).

Eyewitness: The Great Barbados Hurricane of August 11, 1831

"At daylight the wind began to abate, and as it cleared a little, we saw one of the most dreadful sights that the eye could be presented with. Every vessel in the harbour driven on shore but one small schooner... Went into the town which was a terrible sight, the most distressing and melancholy I ever saw. The streets are filled with the fragments of the houses; almost all are unroofed, many thrown flat down even those of brick and stone... Vegetation has almost entirely disappeared, and the spot that yesterday presented one of the most beautiful sights imaginable is now a barren waste."

— Captain George C. McAllister, Journal Entry of August 11, 1831 (Document McAllister016–017)
